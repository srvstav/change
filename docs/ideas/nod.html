    nod

    Parked 2026-10-13: set aside for glass. Working demo lives in ~/code/websockets (uncommitted).

    #The announcement

    "Right now, 40,000 people are staring at the same 'Results will be declared shortly' page as you. They're refreshing it, every one of them alone. Nod is one line of code that lets you see them. A small pill in the corner says '2,314 others here.' Tap it and a nod goes out, and you feel a ripple of nods coming back: from people waiting on the same booking queue at 9:59, people reading the same 3 a.m. error page, people at the last paragraph of the same sad essay. There's no text, no names and no profiles. Nothing is saved, so every nod is gone once it's been felt. It's the glance you share with a stranger on a stuck train, built into any website. Free to embed. Turn off the badge for $5/month."

    #Problem

    • Billions of people are on the same pages at the same moment, and every one of them feels alone. A webpage is a room with no windows.
    • The moments that hurt most are shared ones: results day, the 10 a.m. Tatkal train booking rush in India, concert ticket queues, outage pages, hospital and visa portals, "your call is important to us," a hard docs page late at night. Today people cope by opening a second app (X, Reddit, WhatsApp groups) to ask "is it just me?"
    • The "23 people are viewing this hotel" counters that do exist are used to pressure buyers, and many are fake. Live cursors (Figma, PartyKit's cursor-party) show presence but make the page noisy, and they don't let you say anything.
    • Chat widgets let you say things, but then someone has to moderate them, and a crowd of 40,000 makes chat unreadable.

    #Insight

    • Words don't scale. Gestures do. 40,000 people can't chat, but 40,000 people can nod, and a ripple of "1,203 nods" says more than any message thread. With a fixed set of gestures (nod, 🫂, "same", 🔥), there's nothing to moderate and nothing to translate.
    • The tech's limitation is the product's virtue. Redis pub/sub (the core of the websockets repo) is fire-and-forget: messages aren't stored, and a message sent to nobody just vanishes. Most products work around that. Nod is that. "Nothing is saved" isn't only a privacy policy. It's how the system works, and anyone can check it.
    • Batch the crowd, don't broadcast it. The naive version (your repo's wss.clients.forEach(send)) dies at a big crowd: 10,000 people each nodding once means 100 million messages. Instead, each server adds up its nods every 250 ms and publishes one number per room per tick. Clients get "+37 nods" four times a second. The cost then depends on the number of rooms, not on crowd size squared, so one results page with a million people is no harder than a blog with three readers.
    • Why now: the "dead internet" feeling. With bots and AI slop everywhere, "other real humans are here, right now, feeling this" has become scarce, and people value it.

    #Design sketch

    <script src="https://nod.so/n.js" data-room="auto"></script>   ← room = page URL (hashed), or set by the site

client (≤5 KB gzipped)  pill: "2,314 others here" · tap → gesture picker · ripple animation on incoming batches
gateway (Node + ws)     many instances behind a load balancer; holds sockets; per-room local counters
                        every 250 ms: PUBLISH room:<hash> {here:+n, nods:+k}   (one message per active room)
redis                   per-room channels (SUBSCRIBE when a gateway gets its first socket in that room,
                        UNSUBSCRIBE when its last one leaves; not one global channel like the repo has)
                        presence = per-gateway HINCRBY with a TTL heartbeat, so a dead gateway's
                        counts expire on their own
abuse                   fixed set of gestures; ≤1 gesture per socket per 2 s; per-IP connection cap; no text field

    #Look

    Design canvas: https://claude.ai/artifact/WNZ53mMcy1UZbHJykyaiKS

    • Colors: sky #CFE8FF, lime #E4F26B, mint #BFF0D4, pale lime #F4F9C9, peach #FFD9CF, ink #12304A.
    • Type: Bricolage Grotesque (display), DM Mono (the nerdy labels), Caveat (handwritten notes in the margins).
    • Feel: stickers. Thick ink outlines, hard offset shadows, things slightly tilted. The "o" in the logo is a face with closed eyes.
    • The four gestures: nod, hug, same, spark.

    #Where it plugs in

    Results and exam portals · ticket and booking queues · status and outage pages · 404s · docs and error-code pages · long-form essays (the last paragraph is where people nod) · livestream replays · hospital and visa waiting screens · open-source READMEs ("312 devs reading this right now").

    #Done when

    1. One <script> tag on a blank HTML page shows a live count within 2 s. Bundle ≤ 5 KB gzipped.
    2. A load test reaches 100,000 sockets in one room across 3 gateway instances. Each client receives ≤ 4 messages per second no matter the crowd size, and p95 delay from nod to ripple is < 500 ms.
    3. Kill a gateway mid-test: within 15 s, counts drop by exactly its sockets and the rest keep working.
    4. redis-cli --scan during the test shows only presence counters with TTLs: no stored gestures, ever.
    5. A stranger embeds it on their own site in under 2 minutes using just the README.

    #Time box

    1 week to a public demo (embed + one gateway + Redis). 1 more week for the batching and multi-gateway hardening. If it runs over, cut gestures down to one (the nod), not the batching.

    #Riskiest assumption

    People will tap a nod for strangers, and they'll feel something when nods come back, instead of ignoring the pill. Test (an afternoon): add a nod button and a live count to the existing repo, deploy it on one page with real concurrent traffic (a Show HN, an r/developersIndia thread, or a link dropped in a big Discord during a live event). Measure: % of visitors who send ≥1 nod when ≥5 others are present, and % who nod again after receiving a ripple. Kill if fewer than 10% nod at all. Very promising if more than 30% nod again.

    #Not doing

    • Text, chat, replies, usernames, profiles, follower counts. The moment it has words, it needs moderators.
    • Storing history, "nods over time" charts for visitors, or replays. Ephemeral by design. (Site owners may get aggregate counts, never a per-person record.)
    • Fake or padded counts. The number is always real; that's the whole brand.
    • Live cursors. Positions are noise; a gesture means something.

    #Open questions

    • Who pays? Site owners (badge removal, custom gestures, "peak crowd" stats), or a free consumer browser extension that puts nod on every page, so you can see the crowd anywhere without the site's permission?
    • Room per page URL, or per moment (everyone on any results site at 10:00, everyone hitting the same error code)?

    #Runner-up

    • queue-mates: the same tech, but only for waiting rooms. You get your real place in line, plus the people just ahead of and behind you as anonymous dots, cheering as each one gets through. Narrower, easier to sell to ticketing sites.
    • same-bug: on a docs or error page, "84 people hit this error in the last hour; 12 just fixed it, and here's the one-line fix they chose" (pick from a fixed list). More useful, less human.
