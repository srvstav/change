    rank by evidence, not hype

    Rank AI news by what someone can do with it today and what is actually proven. Let the reader's own ratings, not another model, decide whether the ranking works.

    • Ask the model for facts before scores: what it is, what you can do now, what was released (code, weights, demo, paper, announcement), and what is verified versus only claimed. Then score usefulness against written anchors. "Claims without evidence can't score above 6" does more than any adjective in the prompt.
    • Facts the system found itself beat the model's guess. If there's a GitHub repo, it is not "paper only".
    • Keep the old answer until a new one exists. Dropping old ratings to force a re-rate made good stories sink (radar lost Harness2 this way).
    • The human is the ground truth. Rate Useful / Not useful / Knew it, and paste misses. A miss tells you whether a source is missing or the ranking is wrong.
    • Only switch to a learned ranking if it wins on ratings it never saw (time split). Otherwise you fit noise and call it progress.
    • Plain words everywhere. "12,702 GitHub stars · gaining about 942 a day", not "+942★/day". Name the thing ("Harness2"), not the source's whole headline.

    Related: rank-the-unexpected-separately, free-llm-tiers-are-small.
